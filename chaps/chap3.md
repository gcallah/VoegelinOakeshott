# III. Rationalism


Oakshott's critique of rationalism follows directly from the ideas he expressed
in Experience and its Modes. As my friend James Connolly once said to me in
conversation, Oakshott really only wrote two books: *Experience and its Modes* and
*Inexperience and its Modes*.

Some authors, failing to understand the connection between the earlier book and
the latter essay, actually wish to dismiss the book as some youthful aberration
and focus entirely on Oakshott's work from the 1950s on. (Gorencer.) To do so
ignores the fact that Oakeshott begins his essay "The Voice of Poetry in the
Conversation of Mankind" with a statement that the real world is a world of
experience, or that he opens his last book published in his lifetime, On
History, by discussing the modalities in which we can view the past.
